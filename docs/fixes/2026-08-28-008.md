# 2026-08-28-008 — an email address stored as the caller's name: `name` had no shape gate, no validator, and a prompt that reshaped emails into it

## Symptom
Live call. The caller reported it "seemed unsure of everything I said" despite speaking clearly.
It was not a transcription problem — every transcript in the trace is verbatim and correct. What
went wrong was attribution.

```
agent:  "…is this to do with your job, your home, or immigration?"
caller: "My landlord is trying to evict me."         -> routed, asks for name
caller: "Yeah, it's about my home. He's basically     -> taken as their NAME
         trying to kick me out with little notice."
agent:  "Great — and what's your email address?"
caller: <gives their email>                           -> DISCARDED
agent:  "Sorry, I need to double check something — what's your name again?"
caller: "manos at gmail dot com."                     -> stored as NAME
                                                         "manos@gmail.com"
agent:  "Great — and what's your email address?"
                                                         -> call_abandoned
```

Trace #24–#53. By the end, `caller_profile["name"]` held an email address.

## Root cause
The caller answered the routing question a beat late, after the fast pass had already moved on to
asking their name. That timing is ordinary and unavoidable. Everything after it is not.

**1. `looks_like_field_shape` returned `True` for every utterance when the field was `name`.**
Its comment read "name/preferred_time: no reliable shape signal, rely on `looks_like_tangent`
alone" — and `looks_like_tangent` only catches question-shaped utterances and a fixed prefix list,
neither of which matches a sentence about a landlord. So the fast path's gate had *nothing* to
say, optimistically advanced to the email question, and spawned a background name check.

The reasoning was sound about positive signals — there is no pattern that says "this is a name",
and there mustn't be one, because names vary enormously. But it silently concluded there were no
*negative* signals either, and there are strong ones: an email address is not a name, a phone
number is not a name, a fifteen-word sentence is not a name.

**2. The extraction prompt's symbol conversion is field-agnostic.** "Convert standard spoken-aloud
conventions into their symbol… 'at' -> '@', 'dot' -> '.'" is stated unconditionally. Asked for the
**name** field and given "manos at gmail dot com", the model did exactly as instructed and returned
`manos@gmail.com` — as a name, at confidence 0.6 (trace #45–#46). That is the prompt working as
written, not the model misbehaving.

**3. `apply_extraction` checked confidence and nothing else.** For `name` it is purely
`>= 0.75 confirmed / >= 0.4 pending_confirm / else missing`. Confidence answers "did I hear that
right"; it never answers "is that the kind of thing I asked for". 0.6 on a correctly-heard email
address sailed through and was written to the profile as a name. `email` and `phone` are validated
*above* the bands (`validate_email` / `validate_phone`); `name` had no equivalent.

**4. (found while fixing 1)** `node_capture`'s fresh-extraction branch counted a failed attempt for
`email` and `phone` but not for `name`, which fell through to a plain "Thanks — and what's your
name?" with `attempts` untouched. Verified pre-fix: three consecutive failures, `attempts` still 0,
escalation unreachable. This was nearly unreachable before — the fast path optimistically advanced
past such utterances rather than falling back here — but fixing (1) makes it the *ordinary* path
for "that wasn't a name", so it had to be bounded before the gate fix could ship.

## Fix
**Negative shape signals for `name`** (`heuristics.looks_like_field_shape`): reject an utterance
that sounds like an email (`@`, or " at " and " dot " together), carries seven or more digits, is
a phone number read out in words (five or more number-words — individually ordinary English, so
the count is the test), or runs longer than `MAX_SPOKEN_NAME_WORDS = 12`. A false positive here is
cheap by explicit design — it costs one fallback to the synchronous path, which extracts correctly
anyway — so the bar for adding a signal is low and the ceiling errs high.

**`heuristics.looks_like_a_name`**, checked inside `apply_extraction` before the confidence bands.
Deliberately a *negative* check — it rejects what a name provably isn't rather than defining what
one is, because names are far too varied for a positive pattern and a false rejection costs a
caller an extra re-ask. It lives in `heuristics.py` rather than beside `validate_email` in
`tools.py` because `apply_extraction` is a pure function with no `repos` to route a traced tool
call through (CLAUDE.md rule 8), and because putting it at that single funnel covers all three
extraction call sites at once instead of duplicating a check three ways.

**`prompts.FIELD_GUARDS`**, formatted into both extraction prompts and empty for every field
without an entry, so the email and phone prompts are byte-for-byte unchanged. The `name` entry
turns the "at"/"dot" conversion off for that field specifically (spelled-out letters still
convert — "M A N O S" -> "Manos") and states that an email, a phone number, or a description of
the legal problem is not a name. Backed by the code check above rather than trusted on its own,
per `docs/DECISIONS.md`'s Haiku->Sonnet lesson.

**A failed `name` extraction now goes through `_deny_and_reprompt`**, exactly as email and phone
already did — so it counts an attempt and escalates on the third instead of re-asking forever.

## Verification
`pytest backend/tests eval/tests` — 493 pass (461 before, +32), plus `scripts/check_architecture.py`.
Five of the new tests were confirmed to fail against the pre-fix code, including both live
utterances and `apply_extraction("name", "manos@gmail.com", 0.6)`. The positive sets deliberately
include names the negative signals must not reject — `Jean-Luc`, `O'Brien`, `Ng`,
`María José García`, and the spelled-out `"It's Manos, that's M A N O S"`. The unbounded name
re-ask was reproduced before the fix (three turns, `attempts` still 0) and after (attempts 1, 2,
then `capture_failed`). Not yet re-verified on a live call.

## Noted, not fixed
- **A bare "Hello?" as the caller's first utterance burns a classification attempt.** `node_greeting`
  is content-blind and chains straight into routing, so the opener is always classified; "Hello?"
  correctly returns `unclear`, which counts 1 of the 2 allowed. In practice callers who open that
  way get a single real classification attempt before `unable_to_classify`. Seen in this trace
  (#6–#9) but it did not fire — the caller's next utterance routed cleanly.
- **The delayed-failure re-ask still discards the utterance it interrupts.** That is deliberate and
  documented (`_delayed_failure_reask`), and it is what discarded this caller's email. The gate fix
  removes the common cause of that interrupt rather than changing the interrupt itself.
