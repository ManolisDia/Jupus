# 2026-08-24-008 — a leftover "yep, that's correct" got misattributed as the research intro's answer, silently burning the citation

## Symptom
Live testing: a caller described a real eviction-without-notice situation at the very start of
the call (during routing), went through capture normally, but never got a statute citation — the
call went straight from the research intro question to "what day and time works for you?" with no
citation and no disclaimer, as if research had been skipped entirely.

## Root cause
The trace showed `node_research_gather` ran on the utterance `"Yep, that's correct."` — not a
landlord-situation description. Compared against the immediately preceding turn's utterance
(`"That's correct."`, confirming the phone number), these are two distinct strings, ruling out a
literal duplicate dispatch. The likely mechanism: `_enter_research` (`backend/supervisor/graph.py`)
asks the research intro question in the *same* turn/return as the capture→research transition —
deliberately, for latency (no extra chained turn) — so if the caller is still finishing a reaction
to the *previous* question (confirming their phone number) when the *new* question lands, there's
no gap for them to "catch up." That trailing reaction then arrives as the caller's next utterance
and gets fed straight into `node_research_gather`, which — unlike `node_capture_fast`'s
`looks_like_tangent`/shape-check gates — had no plausibility check at all. Whatever text arrived
was treated as the substantive answer, BM25 correctly found nothing relevant in it, and
`node_research_deliver` correctly said nothing — every downstream step behaved exactly as
designed given its input; the bug was entirely in accepting that input as the real answer in the
first place.

## Fix
`backend/supervisor/heuristics.py` adds `looks_like_bare_affirmation(utterance)` — true when an
utterance's words are *entirely* drawn from a small acknowledgment/affirmation vocabulary ("yes",
"yeah", "correct", "that's", "right", ...) and nothing else. Unlike `looks_like_tangent` (which
flags utterances that read as a *question* or aside), this flags utterances that carry no
substantive content at all — a real answer to "tell me what happened" essentially never consists
purely of these words, even a short one ("he just showed up" already has content words outside the
set).

`node_research_gather` now checks this (after the existing `looks_like_research_skip` check, before
committing to the answer): on a bare affirmation, it re-asks once ("Sorry, I don't think I quite
caught that — can you tell me a bit more about what's actually been happening?") rather than
spawning a search on it; a *second* consecutive bare affirmation gives up gracefully and moves to
booking rather than looping or escalating — this stage is best-effort enrichment only (Decision 4,
`docs/phases/phase-8-legal-research.md`), never allowed to stall or fail the call. Tracked via the
existing `retry_counts["research_gather"]` counter, same mechanism `node_routing` already uses for
its own classification retry.

## Verification
`pytest` (full suite, 295 tests) passes, including direct heuristic tests
(`test_bare_affirmation_detected`, `test_substantive_answers_not_flagged_as_bare_affirmation`) and
graph-level regression tests (`test_research_gather_bare_affirmation_reasks_without_spawning_search`,
`test_research_gather_gives_up_after_second_bare_affirmation`). Not yet re-verified live — the
original repro needs a real call where a caller's confirm-back reaction and the research intro
question overlap in timing again.
